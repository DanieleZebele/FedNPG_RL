\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We reproduced FedNPG-ADMM from scratch and evaluated it across three MuJoCo
environments, four agent counts and five seeds. Swimmer reproduces the
reported final rewards at two, four and eight agents and exceeds them with one,
monotone in $N$, and Humanoid scales
$6.4\times$ from one agent to eight. On Hopper, with a reduced configuration closer to the algorithm as written,
four and eight agents match the reported rewards and learn faster than one or
two, while one and two remain indistinguishable from each other. Against the baselines at eight agents,
FedNPG-ADMM matches standard-average FedNPG on Hopper and exceeds it on
Humanoid, at three to six orders of magnitude less uplink.

We measured the communication argument directly instead of assuming it,
obtaining per-agent per-round reductions of $2434\times$, $9219\times$ and
$363537\times$ on the three environments. FedNPG-ADMM is not the cheapest of
the compared methods in absolute terms, since it transmits twice what FedPPO
does, so its case rests on convergence per round.

Quantizing the agent-to-server transmissions, which the original work does not
attempt, costs little at 8 bits. Across five seeds, all three environments
keep their final reward within the seed spread and their faster learning with
more agents,
for a further $4\times$ against the \texttt{float32} values exchanged
($8\times$ at the paper's 64-bit accounting) on top of the
$\Oh(d^2) \to \Oh(d)$ reduction. Below a
threshold that grows with the policy dimension the collapse is abrupt rather
than graceful: Humanoid fails at 6 bits at every agent count, while Swimmer
holds its final values there but loses the agent-count ordering early in
training, and Hopper still reaches its unquantized result at $N=8$.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.} The missing axis is communication
\emph{frequency}. FedNPG-ADMM optimizes what is transmitted per round, while
FedAvg-style schemes with $E$ local steps optimize how often rounds occur
\citep{jin2022fedrlheterogeneity, khodadadian2022linearspeedup}. The two are
orthogonal and could be combined. A second open question is the Humanoid gap between FedNPG-ADMM and the exact
aggregation it approximates (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}).
