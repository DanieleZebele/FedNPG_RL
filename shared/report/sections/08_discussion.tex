\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our study uses five seeds against the original's ten, so our error bars are
wider than they appear relative to a ten-seed estimate. Hopper is reported
under a different configuration from Swimmer and Humanoid
(Table~\ref{tab:ours}), so comparisons across environments mix the algorithm
with its practical settings. All runs use a single machine and a single
software stack, so implementation-level differences from the authors' setup
cannot be separated from algorithmic ones. That concern has precedent here:
the same algorithm under identical hyperparameters has been shown to differ by
more than a factor of two on Hopper across implementations
\citep{henderson2018matters}. Finally, two hyperparameters ($\eta$ and the
interpretation of Table~4's learning-rate column,
Section~\ref{sec:implementation}) are our reading of an ambiguous source, and
a different reading could change the results, on Hopper in particular.
