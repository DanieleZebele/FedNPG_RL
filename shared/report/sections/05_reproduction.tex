\section{Reproduction results}
\label{sec:reproduction}

We run $N \in \{1,2,4,8\}$ on Swimmer-v4, Hopper-v4 and Humanoid-v4 with five
random seeds per configuration (60 runs). Swimmer and Humanoid use the
configuration listed in Appendix~\ref{app:hyperparameters}; Hopper uses a
reduced one (Section~\ref{sec:implementation}).
Table~\ref{tab:reproduction} reports
the mean of each seed's final 100 iterations, then the mean and standard
deviation across seeds, the same quantity Table~2 of the original reports.

\subsection{Swimmer: reproduced}

Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}(a) shows the curves behind the Swimmer column, and
Figure~\ref{fig:communication}(a) the same runs against uplink traffic. Final
rewards fall within the reported spread at $N = 2, 4, 8$ and above it at
$N=1$ ($123.9$ against $109.4 \pm 5.9$), and they increase monotonically with
$N$, as the paper describes. More agents also learn faster: at iteration 50
the smoothed return is $44$, $62$, $75$ and $105$ for $N = 1, 2, 4, 8$.

\subsection{Humanoid: strong scaling}
\label{sec:humanoid}

Humanoid is absent from the original Table~2, so we have no direct comparison.
The qualitative claim nevertheless holds, since return grows $6.4\times$ from $N=1$ to $N=8$,
monotonically, with the $N=4$ and $N=8$ variance small relative to the mean.

\subsection{Hopper}
\label{sec:hopper}

Four and eight agents reach $2536 \pm 116$ and $2656 \pm 286$
(Table~\ref{tab:reproduction}), within one standard deviation of the reported
$2507$ and $2719$, and learning speed increases with $N$ as the paper
describes. At iteration 100 the smoothed training return in
Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}(b) is $598$, $638$, $986$ and $1092$ for
$N = 1, 2, 4, 8$. The ordering is not strictly monotone throughout: two agents
finish below one ($1636 \pm 282$ against $1941 \pm 494$), and four agents
briefly lead eight around iteration 200. Both gaps are smaller than the seed
spread. What the five seeds resolve is two groups, one and two agents against
four and eight, separated by roughly $700$ at the end of training. Our
single-agent value is above the reported $1473$.
